Noise Space Learning for VLA Adaptation
Authors: Junjie Lu, Chuheng Zhang, Yucen Wang, Yuhua Jiang, Xinyao Qin, Min Xu, Li Zhao
First: 2026-10-09T17:58:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:58:50+00:00

Abstract
Pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) models encode broad manipulation priors, yet adapting them to specific tasks requires balancing limited robot interaction, computational resources, and safety constraints. Noise space learning (NSL) adapts flow-matching-based VLAs by learning a context-conditioned distribution over noise while keeping the pretrained VLA fixed. Building on prior noise-steering methods, we formulate NSL as an interface for VLA adaptation that accommodates different learning algorithms and instantiate it with PPO, SAC, and AWR. Theoretically, we provide an information-theoretic analysis of how NSL enables efficient adaptation. Empirically, we study these NSL algorithms on LIBERO, RoboTwin, and four real-robot manipulation tasks under interaction, computation, and execution constraints. Across these settings, we develop a suite of effective NSL variants and derive practical recommendations for choosing among them under different resource and deployment regimes.

Evolving Hybrid Quantum-Classical Architectures for Image Classification

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Hybrid quantum classical neural networks integrate parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) with established deep learning architectures, but their performance depends strongly on the choice of quantum circuit architecture, a choice that remains largely manual. Most existing approaches rely on hand-designed or fixed circuit ansätze, requiring circuit structure, gate composition, and qubit connectivity to be specified in advance with no guarantee that they suit the task. This limitation is especially acute in image classification, where quantum circuits must transform features extracted by classical networks while remaining compact enough for practical training, requirements that generic, task-agnostic ansätze are unlikely to satisfy simultaneously. We extend EXAQC, an evolutionary framework for automated quantum circuit discovery, to image classification. EXAQC evolves PQCs as intermediate processing modules while retaining classical feature-extraction and prediction layers. On MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10, EXAQC achieves 98.42%, 90.62%, and 85.47% accuracy, respectively, while using comparable gate counts to other quantum architecture-search methods. Against classical networks, evolved hybrid models maintain comparable accuracy with substantially fewer trainable parameters, reaching 85.68% on CIFAR-10 with over 25$\times$ fewer parameters than a 10-layer CNN. Encoding choice also matters: rotation-based encodings (RX, RY, U3) outperform amplitude encoding by 22-25 points on CIFAR-10. These results demonstrate that automated circuit discovery yields compact quantum modules that can replace larger classical components in vision architectures while retaining competitive accuracy.

Chronos Policy: Learning Robot Policies on Physical Time
Authors: Changhao Wang, Jaden Clark, Shuran Song
First: 2026-10-09T17:55:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:55:20+00:00
Comments: Project website: https://changhaowang.github.io/chronospolicy/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Robot policies often convert sensing and control into fixed-rate observation-action sequences, leaving physical time implicit in sequence position. This assumption breaks when observation rates or delays vary, or when inference latency changes. We introduce Chronos Policy, which makes physical time explicit by conditioning on observation capture times and desired action execution times. We study two ways to incorporate time into a diffusion transformer: adding timestamp embeddings to token features, or encoding physical time in positional encoding through time-aware rotary positional encodings (time-aware RoPE). We find that time-aware RoPE is more robust, allowing attention to depend directly on physical time while keeping token content unchanged. During training, we temporally randomize which observations are sampled and when the action chunk begins, exposing the policy to a wider range of sensing and execution schedules that may occur during deployments. This also enables learning from asynchronous, multi-rate data without forcing all measurements onto a fixed temporal grid. Across simulation and real-world experiments, Chronos Policy improves performance on dynamic tasks, where observation-to-action timing is critical, and on multisensory tasks, where the relative timing between observations matters. On standard quasi-static tasks, it preserves nominal performance while improving robustness to changes in sensing and inference timing.

Goal-Oriented Bundle Management and Flow Control for Deep-Space Communications via Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Sajjad Baghaeea, Aimin Lib, Shanmugarajan Balasubramanian, Elif Uysal

Abstract
Deep-space communications operate under intermittent connectivity, long propagation delays, and relay storage constraints. In such networks, the Bundle Protocol enables store-carry-forward communication, requiring relay nodes to manage limited storage and scarce contact opportunities. Relay-side scheduling and dropping decisions are inherently goal-oriented: transmission resources should be allocated to bundles most valuable to downstream mission objectives. Conventional schedulers, such as FIFO, do not explicitly optimize task utility and may provide unfair service across heterogeneous planetary sources. This paper presents a closed-loop modeling and learning framework for relay-side bundle and flow management in a contact-driven deep-space network, where lunar and Martian updates are forwarded through a shared-buffer relay to Earth. We quantify destination-side information freshness using excess age $Δ_i^e(t)=[Δ_i(t)-d_i(t)]^+$, where $Δ_i(t)$ denotes the Age of Information of source $i$ and $d_i(t)$ is a source-dependent delay baseline. Based on this metric, we define a goal-oriented loss that supports source-dependent nonlinear penalties, allowing prioritization according to mission relevance. We formulate relay control as a constrained semi-Markov decision process (CSMDP) and solve it using masked Lagrangian Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). Feasibility-aware action masking restricts transmissions to bundles that can complete within the remaining contact window. DTNSim simulations indicate improved age-based utility and inter-flow fairness compared with baseline schedulers. The proposed framework jointly optimizes relay-side bundle scheduling, dropping, and flow control under contact-driven dynamics with explicit fairness considerations across heterogeneous sources.

Revisiting Tabular Deep Regression: Objectives and the Limits of Global Early Stopping
Authors: Danil Rudenko, Georgii Bulgakov, Nikolay Kartashev, Ivan Rubachev, Daniil Krasylnikov, Artem Babenko
First: 2026-10-09T17:49:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T17:49:32+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
While deep learning models for tabular data have recently received significant research attention, the choice of objective functions for regression tasks remains an understudied degree of freedom. Several lines of research offer alternatives to the standard Mean Squared Error (MSE), but differences in datasets, backbones, and evaluation protocols make their practical benefits difficult to compare. In this work, we address this gap by performing a systematic re-evaluation of regression objectives across a broad collection of established tabular datasets and multiple neural backbones. Our comparison reveals that many proposed objectives provide limited or inconsistent gains over a properly tuned MSE baseline, while quantile loss yields consistent aggregate improvements over MSE across the tested backbones. We then identify a limitation that remains even with this strong objective: regions with different levels of target noise can reach their best predictive performance at different training epochs. Consequently, a single global early-stopping checkpoint can impose a compromise between uncertainty regions, even when the objective can represent heteroskedastic target distributions. Motivated by this limitation, we introduce a heteroskedasticity-aware extension of quantile loss that uses input-dependent label smoothing as a potential remedy and improves over quantile loss on several backbones. Overall, our results provide a modern empirical picture of regression objectives for tabular deep learning and highlight how differences in optimal stopping times across uncertainty regions can limit the effectiveness of global early stopping. The code is available at https://github.com/yandex-research/tabular-regression.

Abstract
Many optimization problems provide direct access to only part of the gradient, while the remaining component must be inferred from function evaluations. This structure arises in performative prediction, performative reinforcement learning, and bilevel optimization with a black-box lower level. We introduce Partially Informed Stochastic Optimization (PISO), which combines the available gradient component with a running estimate of the residual. By centring each zeroth-order observation on this estimate, PISO makes the variance due to random probing depend on the tracking error rather than the residual's magnitude. This yields a randomized sketch iteration with an explicit contraction mechanism and a principled interpretation of momentum. For smooth nonconvex objectives, we establish convergence guarantees that depend on the residual's variation and initial magnitude, without requiring a uniform bound on the unavailable gradient component. With independently sampled function evaluations and Gaussian or spherical probes, PISO attains leading function-evaluation complexities of \(O(d^{2}\varepsilon^{-6})\) under Lipschitz gradients and \(O(d^{2}\varepsilon^{-5})\) under an additional Lipschitz-Hessian assumption. These rates require only one probe direction, two function samples, and one known-component sample per update. The analysis also quantifies how batching trades observations for fewer deployments. Across five settings, PISO variants improve on the tested baselines with comparable oracle access in three, reach a comparable solution faster in bilevel routing, and perform best among methods without response-model derivatives in performative reinforcement learning.

Abstract
Nearly every autonomous or semi-autonomous flipper controller assumes access to an elevation map of the terrain under and in front of the robot. The terrain beneath the chassis is occluded in the sensors, so the map is remembered through odometry integration, whose drift corrupts it. We present Stabilizing Anti-Stuck (SAS), a mapless controller that combines a stabilization term for body roll and pitch with an anti-stuck term that frees the robot when it gets stuck. As input, SAS uses the IMU, the flipper joint angles, and a motion cue that only tells whether the robot is moving. The cue is computed over a short window and never accumulated, so SAS does not inherit the odometry-integration drift that corrupts the map. We deploy SAS on a tracked robot with four flippers and evaluate it against manual teleoperation and prior flipper controllers in a large comparison, both in simulation and on a real obstacle. In simulation and on the real robot, SAS achieves traversal quality comparable to state-of-the-art map-based controllers. On a log hidden in tall grass, invisible to the LiDAR, the best map-based controller gets stuck while SAS traverses the hidden obstacle without a problem.

Abstract
Robots deployed in realistic settings will accumulate experience across many sessions and tasks over their deployment. The robot's tasks may often require it to remember information from multiple sessions ago, making long-context robot memory important for real-world deployments. However, most robot-memory benchmarks today are based on single episodes or a short context. To measure how current robot memory systems perform on longer sessions with more distractions, we introduce RoboMME-Interference, a cross-session benchmark built on RoboMME (Dai et al., 2026). For each query episode, we construct a session history using the query's relevant prior demonstration followed by a controlled number of unrelated sessions, which we provide to the VLA as memory and measure accuracy. Running RoboMME's released memory-augmented $π_{0.5}$ variants unmodified through this benchmark, we find that while perceptual memory variants improve success when given the history without any distractors, they decay strongly and steadily as unrelated sessions accumulate. The subgoal variants, which read the history with a vision-language model and pass written subgoals to the policy, improve less at their best but hold more of that improvement as distractors accumulate. Adding a retrieval step to the strongest perceptual variant, which selects the section of history most visually similar to the robot's current view and passes only that section to the policy, restores its no-distractor success rate at every interference level. With this release, we emphasize the importance of long-context memory and robustness to interference and show that current systems largely fail on such capabilities. The project page, videos, code, and data are at https://robotmemorybench.com.

Abstract
We investigate the theoretical aspects of offline reinforcement learning (RL) under general function approximation. While prior works (e.g., Xie et al., 2021) have established the theoretical foundations of learning a good policy from offline data via pessimism, existing algorithms that are computationally tractable (often in an oracle-efficient sense), such as PSPI, only apply to finite and small action spaces. Moreover, these algorithms rely on state-wise mirror descent and require actors to be implicitly induced from the critic functions, failing to accommodate standalone policy parameterization which is ubiquitous in practice. In this work, we address these limitations and extend the theoretical guarantees to parameterized policy classes over large or continuous action spaces. When extending mirror descent to parameterized policies, we identify contextual coupling as the core difficulty, and show how connecting mirror descent to natural policy gradient leads to novel analyses, guarantees, and algorithmic insights, including a surprising unification between offline RL and imitation learning.

Abstract
Generalist robot policies are trained with imitation learning and are bounded by the quality and coverage of their demonstrations. Reinforcement learning can improve and extend them, either by fine-tuning the policy weights or by training a small module to steer the input noise of a frozen generative policy. When tasks arrive sequentially, both approaches suffer from catastrophic forgetting. Fine-tuning on a task degrades prior tasks, and steering only shifts forgetting to the shared steering module. We propose Dream to Remember (D2R), which continually steers a frozen generalist policy toward new tasks, while retaining its earlier skills. As the steering module learns a new task, a frozen pretrained world model rehearses prior tasks through imagined rollouts, anchoring the module to its earlier behavior without a replay buffer or new data. When the world model is faithful enough, D2R learns the new task entirely in imagination. Otherwise, it learns the task in the environment while using the world model only to rehearse prior tasks. On CALVIN, D2R improves eight tasks in sequence entirely in imagination, raising average success from 63.7% to 74.8%, while LoRA fine-tuning on the same sequence drops it to 11.7%. Abstract
LiDAR-inertial odometry (LIO) is widely used for real-time localization and mapping, but its reliability can degrade substantially in subterranean environments affected by smoke, dust, and other airborne obscurants, whereas radar remains comparatively robust. This article presents a one-class temporal learning framework for LiDAR degradation detection and reliability-aware localization under such conditions. A broad set of degradation-sensitive cues, including spatial, cross-modal, geometric/motion, intensity, and measurement-recency features, is evaluated to characterize changes in LiDAR sensing quality. Their temporal evolution is modelled over sliding windows using an LSTM autoencoder trained exclusively on nominal operation, enabling deviations from healthy sensor behaviour to be detected without requiring labelled degradation data. The resulting LiDAR reliability estimate governs adaptive selection between LIO and radar inertial odometry (RIO), retaining LIO during reliable sensing and transferring localization authority to RIO when degradation is detected. The framework is evaluated using real-world ground robot data collected in GNSS-denied subterranean tunnels under nominal and dense-smoke conditions. Experimental results demonstrate degradation detection, adaptive modality switching, and continued localization output under severe LiDAR degradation. A feature family ablation study further shows that a reduced feature representation can improve degradation-aware localization performance in the evaluated dense-smoke scenarios, highlighting the importance of feature parsimony in one-class sensor degradation detection. The code will be released as open source to support reproducibility and further research.

Abstract
Recent systems train coding agents with reinforcement learning (RL) on trajectories collected through multiple execution harnesses. However, gains under the training harnesses leave open whether the improvements transfer to unseen interfaces and how they depend on reward normalization. We present a controlled study that measures RL gains under fixed harnesses and compares the same models across harnesses. We train two Qwen3-8B RL variants from the same supervised fine-tuning (SFT) checkpoint using identical replay data. They differ only in whether rewards for each task are normalized within each harness (Within) or across harnesses (Cross). On SWE-bench Verified, we identify three main findings: (i) Multi-harness RL yields modest gains over the SFT baseline under both normalization rules. Across the four training harnesses, the estimated mean improvements are +0.48 and +0.77 percentage points for Within and Cross, respectively. (ii) Cross increases the fraction of trajectories with nonzero advantage but provides no consistent advantage over Within on an unseen harness across training seeds and evaluation budgets; neither rule significantly improves over SFT on this harness. (iii) Performance varies substantially more across harnesses than with RL training. After averaging over model variants, the highest and lowest source-harness solve rates differ by 7.13 percentage points, far exceeding the estimated training gains. Even after multi-harness RL, the observed performance differences across execution interfaces remain substantially larger than either the gains from training or the differences between normalization rules.

Abstract

Abstract
For fully actuated rigid robots, kinematic inversion is a purely geometric problem, efficiently solved by closed-loop inverse kinematics (CLIK) schemes that compute joint velocities to position the robot body in space. For underactuated soft robots, however, not all configurations are attainable through control action, making kinematic inversion extremely ill-posed. Extensions of CLIK address this by introducing end-to-end mappings from actuation to task space for the controller to operate on, but typically assume finite dimensions of the underlying virtual configuration space. In this work, we formulate CLIK in the infinite-dimensional domain to reason about the entire soft robot shape while solving tasks, thereby skipping intermediate approximation steps. We do this by composing an actuation-to-shape map with a shape-to-task map, deriving the differential end-to-end kinematics via an infinite-dimensional chain rule, and thereby obtaining a Jacobian-based CLIK algorithm. Since complex soft-robot models are rarely available in closed form, and neither the actuation-to-shape mapping nor its Jacobian can typically be obtained analytically, we propose to learn it using differentiable neural operator networks. We first present an analytical study on a constant-curvature segment, and then apply the neural version of the algorithm to a three-fiber soft robotic arm whose underlying model relies on morphoelasticity and active filament theory.

Abstract
Safe exploration remains a fundamental challenge in reinforcement learning (RL), limiting the deployment of RL agents in the real world. We propose Sampling-Based Safe Reinforcement Learning (SBSRL), a model-based RL algorithm that maintains safety throughout the learning process by enforcing constraints jointly across a finite set of dynamics samples. This formulation approximates an intractable worst-case optimization over uncertain dynamics and enables practical safety guarantees in continuous domains. We further introduce an exploration strategy based on constraining epistemic uncertainty, eliminating the need for explicit exploration bonuses. Under regularity conditions, we derive high-probability guarantees of safety throughout learning and a finite-time sample complexity bound for recovering a near-optimal policy. Empirically, SBSRL achieves safe and efficient exploration both in simulation and in real robotic hardware, and readily extends to practical deep-ensemble implementations that scale to high-dimensional continuous control problems.

Abstract
Harvesting technologies have evolved from conventional mechanized systems based on bulk mechanical interaction toward increasingly automated and robotic systems capable of perception, decision-making, and localized actuation. This evolution has also created a classification problem, as harvesting systems are commonly described according to crop type, mechanical removal principle, sensing technology, robotic architecture, or degree of automation, making direct comparison difficult. This paper proposes a unified taxonomy for agricultural harvesting technologies based on two primary classification axes: the physical principle governing plant-machine interaction and the level of automation incorporated into the harvesting process. Selectivity is treated as an emergent functional attribute that expresses the biological and spatial scale at which perception and decision-making influence physical actuation. Conventional mechanized methods, including cutting, pulling, vibration, impact, brushing, pneumatic action, and combined systems, are analyzed together with semi-automated and robotic harvesting approaches. The analysis shows that increasing automation does not necessarily result in greater selectivity. A qualitative transition occurs when perception and decision-making directly influence product-level actuation, shifting harvesting from distributed or bulk interaction toward localized and selective manipulation. The proposed framework provides a common conceptual basis for positioning and comparing mechanized, automated, and robotic harvesting technologies. By separating the physical mechanism of product removal from the automation level and resulting selectivity, the taxonomy clarifies technological boundaries, identifies intermediate configurations, and supports the analysis and development of future harvesting systems.

Abstract
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has substantially improved LLM reasoning, yet growing evidence suggests it mainly performs distribution sharpening: concentrating probability mass on reasoning paths the base model already produces. Recent work therefore seeks RLVR's gains without needing reward signals, by sampling from the power distribution $π_α\propto π_{\text{base}}^α$ via Markov chain Monte Carlo or sequential Monte Carlo. We show that these samplers fail to converge to $π_α$ under realistic compute budgets, and that their gains are largely recreated via simple Best-of-$N$ with appropriate grading . This suggests they succeed by selecting high-probability sequences, not by approximating $π_α$, raising a natural question: is the power distribution itself a worthwhile target? We test this hypothesis directly by fine-tuning the base model to match $π_α$, sidestepping sampler convergence issues entirely. Despite approximating the target far more faithfully than existing samplers, this strategy yields gains that are noisy and specific to certain models and sampling tasks. Together, our results suggest that the value of power sampling lies not in faithfully targeting $π_α$, but in the simpler goal of extracting sequences the base model already deems likely.

Abstract
Turbulent boundary layers over aerodynamic surfaces are a major source of aircraft drag, yet their control remains challenging due to multiscale dynamics and spatial variability, particularly under adverse pressure gradients. Reinforcement learning has outperformed state-of-the-art strategies in canonical flows, but its application to realistic geometries is limited by computational cost and transferability. Here we show that these limitations can be overcome by exploiting local structures of wall-bounded turbulence. Policies are trained in turbulent channel flows matched to wing boundary-layer statistics and deployed directly onto a NACA4412 wing section at $Re_c=2\times10^5$ without additional training on the wing, constituting zero-shot deployment. The transferred policies reduce total drag by 11.1\%, while state-of-the-art opposition control reduces it by 8.9\%. With the actuation cost taken into account, the corresponding net-energy savings are 10.9\% and 8.8\%, respectively. Surrogate training provides an estimated improvement of four orders of magnitude in exploration efficiency over direct on-wing training, enabling scalable flow control.

Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) policies issue sparse targets whose arrival at the robot controller is delayed and irregular, so a reasonable predicted action can still produce abrupt motion near people. Remedies are evaluated on different streams, delays and metrics. JitterVLA replays identical commands and arrival traces through alternative controllers, isolating the controller's effect. We apply it to synthetic reaches, hand trajectories from 20 people, three open VLA pipelines (838 episodes) and a physical UR3. Interpolation lowered modeled p99 jerk for every source, by 83% (615 to 106 m/s^3) on human motion with 200 ms delay, at a 2.0 cm tracking cost, and the effect replicated on 598 new episodes; jerk-limited generation lowered jerk further at a larger cost. Entropy-discontinuity associations had opposite, replicated signs in two pipelines, and entropy gating helped in one but not the other. On the UR3, interpolation lowered accelerometer-derived jerk by 3.0-14.0% in all six exploratory pairs, while a position-derived metric did not resolve the contrast. JitterVLA supports screening before human-centred studies; code and data will be released.
